# Brief 01 · The big picture: language modeling

- File: `pages/01-big-picture.html` · Part I · Foundations · P0 · ~45 min · body 4,500–6,500 words
- Prereqs: none (first page). Link forward generously.
- Drill module name: `lm_loss`

## Purpose
Give a reader who knows ML but not LLMs the complete mental model: what a language model
computes, how it is trained, how it is used, and the three-stage pipeline (pretrain → SFT →
RL) that the rest of the course expands. This page sets vocabulary for everything after it.

## Must cover
1. **Autoregressive factorization** \(p(x_{1:T}) = \prod_t p(x_t \mid x_{<t})\); a model maps
   a token prefix to a distribution over the vocabulary via logits and softmax. Contrast with
   masked LM (BERT) and span corruption (T5). Chinese terms: 自回归, 语言模型.
2. **Training objective**: next-token cross-entropy = maximum likelihood; teacher forcing;
   the one-position shift between inputs and labels; every position is a training example
   (why decoder-only training is sample-efficient per sequence); ignore-index masking for
   padding.
3. **Metrics**: loss in nats, **perplexity** \(= e^{\text{loss}}\) (困惑度) and its "effective
   branching factor" reading; why perplexity is not comparable across tokenizers;
   **bits per byte** (and bits per character) as the tokenizer-independent alternative:
   \(\text{bpb} = \text{loss}_{\text{nats/token}} \cdot \frac{\text{tokens}}{\text{bytes}} / \ln 2\).
   Worked example with explicit numbers (for example: loss 2.0 nats/token, 4.0 bytes/token).
4. **Exposure bias**: the train/inference mismatch of teacher forcing, stated briefly.
5. **Architectural families and why decoder-only won**: encoder-only (BERT), encoder-decoder
   (T5), decoder-only (GPT). Arguments: training signal at every position, one uniform stack,
   KV-cache-friendly generation, prompt-as-interface / in-context learning, empirical scaling
   evidence. Include the nuance from Wang et al. 2022 (what architecture/objective works best
   for zero-shot generalization) that encoder-decoder models with the right objective can be
   competitive; decoder-only won on generality and simplicity at scale.
6. **The pipeline**: pretraining (base model, trillions of tokens, predicts web text) → SFT
   (instruction following, chat format) → preference optimization / RL (RLHF, RLVR for
   reasoning). One paragraph each, plus what each stage costs relative to the others and what
   capability it adds. Use Llama 3 or DeepSeek-V3/R1 as the concrete example (verify token
   counts and stage descriptions). Link to pages 12–14, 19, 21–24.
7. **Base vs instruct vs reasoning models**: what each outputs, how you prompt each.
8. **In-context learning** and few-shot prompting (GPT-3), and the "emergent abilities"
   debate (Wei et al. 2022 vs Schaeffer et al. 2023), briefly and neutrally.
9. **How an LLM is served**, at the level of a diagram: text → tokens (page 02) → embedding →
   L transformer blocks (pages 03–05) → logits → sampling (page 06) → next token, appended
   and repeated. Point out prefill vs decode, deferring detail to page 06.
10. **A course map**: a figure or table mapping each later part to where it sits in the
    pipeline.

## Required interview questions (12–16 total; include these)
- Why is next-token cross-entropy the MLE objective? Derive it.
- Why can't you compare perplexities of models with different tokenizers? What do you use instead?
- What is teacher forcing, and what is exposure bias?
- Why did decoder-only architectures win over encoder-decoder for general LLMs?
- What does each post-training stage add that pretraining lacks?
- "A model's validation loss dropped from 2.10 to 2.05 nats/token. Is that a big improvement?"
  (Discuss perplexity ratio, scale of typical gains, noise, and downstream relevance.)
- What is in-context learning, and why is it surprising?

## Drill: `exercises/drills/lm_loss.py`
- `shift_for_lm(tokens, pad_id)` → (inputs, labels) with labels = tokens shifted left and
  padding (and the final position) set to `-100`.
- `lm_cross_entropy(logits, labels, ignore_index=-100)`: mean NLL over non-ignored positions,
  computed with a numerically stable log-softmax written by hand (logsumexp trick), not
  `F.cross_entropy`. Tests compare against `F.cross_entropy` and include huge logits (1e4) to
  test stability.
- `perplexity(mean_nll)` and `bits_per_byte(total_nll_nats, n_bytes)`.
