# Brief 02 · Tokenization

- File: `pages/02-tokenization.html` · Part I · Foundations · P0 · ~50 min · body 4,500–7,000 words
- Prereqs: 01. Drill module name: `bpe`

## Purpose
Tokenization is where many LLM quirks come from, and a classic interview topic ("walk me
through BPE", "why can't the model count the r's in strawberry"). The reader must be able to
implement BPE from scratch and reason about vocabulary-size trade-offs quantitatively.

## Must cover
1. **Why subwords**: characters (long sequences), words (huge vocab, OOV), subwords as the
   compromise. Tokens per byte / per word as the key efficiency number. Chinese: 分词器, 词表.
2. **BPE training algorithm** (Sennrich et al. 2016) step by step, with a hand-worked toy
   example (small corpus, show 3–5 merges with counts). Encoding by applying merges in rank
   order. Complexity and the standard incremental-count optimization.
3. **Byte-level BPE** (GPT-2): base alphabet of 256 bytes, no unknown tokens, the
   bytes-to-unicode mapping trick, and **pre-tokenization** (regex split before BPE so merges
   never cross word/number/punctuation boundaries). Show the GPT-2 regex and explain what it
   does. Mention later patterns (GPT-4's cl100k splits numbers into 1–3 digit groups; Llama 1/2
   split all digits individually; verify each claim).
4. **WordPiece** (BERT: likelihood-based merge score) and **Unigram LM** (Kudo 2018: start big,
   prune by likelihood loss, sampling-based subword regularization); **SentencePiece** as a
   library that treats text as a raw stream with "▁" for spaces, supporting both BPE and Unigram.
5. **Vocabulary size trade-offs** with numbers: embedding + unembedding parameters \(2Vd\)
   (untied) and their share of the model (Llama-3-8B: \(2 \cdot 128{,}256 \cdot 4096 \approx
   1.05\)B of 8.03B); softmax/logit cost \(2 V d\) FLOPs per token; better compression means
   shorter sequences and so less compute and more context; rare tokens are undertrained.
   A table of vocab sizes for GPT-2, Llama 2, Llama 3, Mistral, Qwen2.5/3, Gemma, DeepSeek-V3,
   each verified from the paper or config.json (note config `vocab_size` can exceed the
   tokenizer size because of padding for hardware efficiency).
6. **Multilingual fertility**: the same sentence costs very different token counts across
   languages, affecting cost and context; why Llama 3 grew its vocab from 32K to 128K
   (verify what the paper says about compression gains).
7. **Special tokens and chat templates** at an intro level (BOS/EOS, pad, role markers);
   deeper treatment on page 19.
8. **Failure modes**: arithmetic and digit splitting; leading-space variants of the same word;
   trailing-whitespace prompts; letter counting ("strawberry"); **glitch tokens**
   ("SolidGoldMagikarp", under-trained tokens; cite Land & Bartolo 2024 on finding
   under-trained tokens, verify ID); tokenizer/model mismatches when fine-tuning.
9. **Beyond tokenizers**: byte-level models (ByT5), Byte Latent Transformer (patches by
   entropy), and why they remain niche. Short section, cite primary sources.

## Required interview questions (12–16 total; include these)
- Walk me through training BPE. How do you encode new text with it?
- Why byte-level BPE instead of character-level BPE?
- How would you choose the vocabulary size for a new model? What changes if you double it?

## Drill: `exercises/drills/bpe.py`
- `train_bpe(text: str, vocab_size: int) -> list[tuple[int, int]]`: byte-level BPE (base 256
  byte ids), returns merges in order; new token id = 256 + merge index. Deterministic tie
  rule: highest count wins; ties broken by the smallest (id1, id2) pair. No pre-tokenization
  (operate on the whole byte string) to keep it simple; say so in the docstring.
- `encode(text, merges) -> list[int]` (apply merges by rank, as in minbpe/tiktoken) and
  `decode(ids, merges) -> str`.
- Tests: round-trip on unicode text (including Chinese and emoji), a small hand-verified
  example with known merges, `vocab_size=256` returns no merges, and encoding is never longer
  than the byte length.
