# Brief 04 · The Transformer block, params & FLOPs

- File: `pages/04-transformer-block.html` · Part I · Foundations · P0 · ~70 min · body 6,000–8,000 words
- Prereqs: 01, 03. Drill module name: `transformer`

## Purpose
Assemble the full decoder-only model and make the reader fluent in the two calculations every
interview asks for: exact parameter counts and FLOPs (6ND). Explain each modern design choice
(pre-norm, RMSNorm, SwiGLU, no biases, untied embeddings) with its reason and evidence.

## Must cover
1. **The residual stream view**: embedding → L blocks that each *add* to the stream →
   final norm → unembedding. Each block = attention sublayer + FFN sublayer. Why residual
   connections matter for depth. Chinese: 残差连接, 前馈网络, 层归一化.
2. **Post-LN vs Pre-LN**: formulas for both; why pre-norm trains stably without warmup
   tricks (Xiong et al. 2020: gradient norms near the output at init); the cost (the
   residual stream's norm grows with depth, some representational concerns); modern variants
   briefly (sandwich/peri-norm, OLMo 2's reordering; deeper treatment on page 11).
3. **LayerNorm vs RMSNorm** (Zhang & Sennrich 2019): formulas, dropping mean-centering and
   bias, speed argument, why it works about as well. Epsilon placement.
4. **FFN**: ReLU/GELU MLP with \(d_{ff} = 4d\); **gated linear units**, SwiGLU (Shazeer
   2020): \(\text{FFN}(x) = (\text{SiLU}(xW_1) \odot xW_3) W_2\); why \(d_{ff} \approx
   \tfrac{8}{3} d\) keeps parameters equal to a 4d MLP; Llama's rounding (Llama-3-8B uses
   14,336; explain the multiplier/rounding logic after verifying it in the reference code or
   paper). Evidence for GLU variants (Shazeer 2020 table).
5. **Embeddings and the LM head**: token embedding lookup; weight tying (Press & Wolf 2016),
   why small models tie and large ones often untie (verify examples: GPT-2 tied, Llama 3
   untied, Gemma tied); no learned positional table when RoPE is used.
6. **Biases and dropout**: why modern LLMs drop linear biases (Llama, PaLM) and use no
   dropout in pretraining (single-epoch data regime).
7. **Initialization**: std 0.02 normal; GPT-2's residual-projection scaling by
   \(1/\sqrt{2L}\); point to μP on page 14.
8. **Exact parameter count** of Llama-3-8B from its config, line by line, reaching
   **8,030,261,248** (embeddings 525,336,576 × 2 untied; per layer: attention 41,943,040 (GQA:
   \(d^2 + 2 \cdot d \cdot 1024 + d^2\)), MLP 176,160,768, two RMSNorms 8,192; 32 layers;
   final norm 4,096). Also the MHA/no-GQA general formula \(\approx 12 L d^2\) for a 4d GELU
   MLP and why it is only a rule of thumb.
9. **FLOPs**: forward ≈ 2N FLOPs per token (each parameter does one multiply-add);
   backward ≈ 2× forward, so training ≈ **6N FLOPs per token** and \(C \approx 6ND\) (Kaplan
   et al. 2020 §2.1); the attention term \(\approx 2 \cdot 2 L T d\) per token forward (for
   \(QK^\top\) and \(AV\), causal halving aside) and when it matters; embeddings excluded from
   N by convention (and why). Worked: training Llama-3-8B on 15T tokens:
   \(6 \times 8.03\text{e}9 \times 15\text{e}12 \approx 7.2\text{e}23\) FLOPs; convert to
   GPU-hours at a stated MFU on H100 (show assumptions). Verify against the paper's reported
   compute if available.
10. **Activation memory** (brief, forward link to page 15): what must be stored for backward
    per layer, why it scales with \(B \cdot T \cdot d\) and with \(T^2\) for naive attention.
11. **A complete minimal GPT** (~120–150 lines) in the page or linked drill: RMSNorm, causal
    MHA (use `F.scaled_dot_product_attention`), SwiGLU MLP, pre-norm block, embedding, final
    norm, LM head, cross-entropy loss. Positions: use a learned absolute table here and say
    page 05 replaces it with RoPE.
