# Brief 08 · Mixture of Experts

- File: `pages/08-mixture-of-experts.html` · Part II · Modern architecture · P0 · ~75 min · body 6,500–8,500 words
- Prereqs: 04, 07. Drill module name: `moe`

## Purpose
MoE is how frontier open models scale parameters without scaling per-token FLOPs, and
DeepSeekMoE plus auxiliary-loss-free balancing are among the most-asked-about DeepSeek ideas.
The reader must be able to write a router and MoE layer, derive the load-balancing loss,
explain why balancing matters (compute and communication), and reason about expert
parallelism and serving costs.

## Must cover
1. **Why MoE**: decouple total parameters (capacity, knowledge) from activated parameters
   (FLOPs per token). Vocabulary: expert, router/gate, top-k, total vs activated params,
   capacity, granularity, sparsity. Chinese: 混合专家, 路由, 负载均衡, 专家并行, 激活参数.
2. **History in one pass**: Shazeer et al. 2017 (noisy top-k gating, importance and load
   losses) → GShard (top-2, expert capacity, aux loss, groups) → Switch Transformer (top-1,
   capacity factor, simplified aux loss, selective fp32 router) → ST-MoE (router z-loss,
   stability and fine-tuning recipe) → Mixtral 8x7B (8 experts, top-2, 47B total / 13B
   active; routing analysis showing weak domain specialization) → DeepSeekMoE → DeepSeek-V2/V3
   → Qwen3 MoE, Llama 4, gpt-oss, Kimi K2 (verify each config you cite).
3. **The router math**: logits \(s = x W_r\); softmax-then-top-k vs top-k-then-softmax;
   sigmoid affinities with normalization over the selected experts (DeepSeek-V3); combining
   expert outputs weighted by gates; shared experts always active.
4. **Load balancing** (负载均衡), the heart of the page:
   - Why imbalance hurts: dead/overloaded experts (routing collapse), wasted capacity, and in
     expert parallelism the slowest device sets the step time.
   - Switch aux loss \(\mathcal{L}_{aux} = \alpha N \sum_i f_i P_i\): define \(f_i\)
     (fraction of tokens dispatched, non-differentiable) and \(P_i\) (mean router
     probability, differentiable); show it is minimized by uniform routing and where the
     gradient flows.
   - Capacity factor, token dropping, overflow handling; "dropless" MoE (MegaBlocks
     block-sparse kernels).
   - Router z-loss (ST-MoE) and fp32 routers for stability.
   - DeepSeek-V2's expert-level, device-level and communication balance losses with the
     coefficients used (verify).
   - **Auxiliary-loss-free balancing** (Wang et al. 2024; DeepSeek-V3): a per-expert bias
     added to affinities *only for top-k selection* (not for gate values), updated after each
     step by ±γ depending on over/under-load; why this avoids the interference gradient that an
     aux loss adds; the small complementary sequence-wise aux loss (α = 0.0001 in V3); V3 drops
     no tokens. Evidence: V3's ablation tables and the expert-specialization figure (verify §4.5.2
     numbers).
   - Global-batch vs micro-batch balancing (Qwen's "Demons in the detail" paper; verify ID).
5. **DeepSeekMoE** (Dai et al. 2024): fine-grained expert segmentation (split each expert
   into m smaller ones and activate m× as many: same FLOPs, far more routing combinations; do
   the combinatorics, e.g. C(16,2) vs C(64,8)) and shared-expert isolation (common knowledge
   in always-on experts, reducing redundancy among routed experts). Evidence vs GShard at equal
   total and activated params.
6. **Expert parallelism** (专家并行): experts sharded across devices; the all-to-all dispatch
   and combine; communication volume per token; why DeepSeek limits each token to M devices or
   nodes (V2: 3 devices; V3: 4 nodes, via group-limited routing: 8 groups, top-4 groups);
   overlap of communication with compute (forward link to 27's DualPipe); grouped GEMMs;
   serving: all experts must be resident in memory, per-expert batch sizes at decode, redundant
   experts for hot spots (V3 §3.4).
7. **Scaling and design choices**: granularity and sparsity scaling (Clark et al. 2022;
   Krajewski et al. 2024; Kimi K2's sparsity-48 law), how many experts / top-k / shared experts
   modern models pick (a verified comparison table: Mixtral, DeepSeek-V2/V3, Qwen3-235B-A22B,
   Llama 4 Maverick/Scout, gpt-oss-120b, Kimi K2), dense first layers (V3's first 3 layers are
   dense: why), sparse upcycling from dense checkpoints (Komatsuzaki et al. 2022).
8. **Worked numbers for DeepSeek-V3**: per-expert params \(3 \cdot 7168 \cdot 2048\);
   routed + shared experts per MoE layer; total and activated params per MoE layer; reconcile
   roughly with 671B / 37B (attention and embeddings from page 07/04). Mixtral 8x7B totals.
   Show arithmetic; compute with python.

## Required interview questions (13–16; include these)
- Why does MoE let you scale parameters without scaling FLOPs? What does it cost instead?
- Derive the Switch load-balancing loss and explain where its gradient flows.
- What is routing collapse and how do you prevent it?
- Explain DeepSeek-V3's auxiliary-loss-free balancing. Why is it better than an aux loss?
- Why fine-grained experts plus shared experts?
- What is the capacity factor, and what happens to dropped tokens?
- Walk through expert parallelism communication for one MoE layer. Why limit the number of nodes per token?
- Compute DeepSeek-V3's activated parameters per token from its config (approximately).
- Serving an MoE: why is memory the constraint, and how does batch size interact with experts?
- MoE vs dense at equal training FLOPs vs equal inference memory: which wins when?

## Drill: `exercises/drills/moe.py`
- `topk_router(x, W_r, k, score="softmax"|"sigmoid", bias=None)` → (indices, gates); bias
  affects selection only; sigmoid gates are normalized over selected experts.
- `MoELayer` (nn.Module): n_routed experts (SwiGLU), n_shared experts, top-k; a correct
  dispatch (loop over experts with index_select / index_add, or sort-based); tests compare to a
  naive per-token loop.
- `switch_aux_loss(router_probs, expert_indices, n_experts)`.
- `update_balance_bias(bias, expert_load, gamma)`: aux-loss-free update.
- `capacity_dispatch_mask(expert_indices, n_experts, capacity)`: which (token, slot) pairs
  survive a capacity limit (first-come order), for understanding token dropping.
