# Brief 09 · Beyond full attention: sparse, linear, SSM, hybrid

- File: `pages/09-beyond-full-attention.html` · Part II · Modern architecture · P1 (P0 for the reader's long-context project) · ~75 min · body 6,500–8,500 words
- Prereqs: 03, 05, 06, 07. Drill module name: `efficient_attention`

## Purpose
The reader's main research project is an efficient long-context language model, so this page
must go deeper than a survey: the math of each family, what it trades away (usually
associative recall), and why 2025–2026 frontier open models converged on hybrids and learned
sparse attention rather than pure linear models. Include the latest verified developments
(as of September 2026; search for anything newer than your training data).

## Must cover
1. **The cost landscape**: full attention is \(O(T^2)\) compute in training/prefill and
   \(O(T)\) KV memory and per-step reads in decode. A table of per-token decode cost and
   state size for each family below.
2. **Local and sliding-window attention**: Longformer; Mistral 7B's sliding window with a
   rolling-buffer cache and the receptive-field-grows-with-depth argument; interleaved
   local/global layers (Gemma 2 alternating; Gemma 3 5:1 local:global with a 1024 window;
   gpt-oss alternating dense and 128-token banded windows); verify every number. What SWA
   breaks (exact long-range retrieval in local layers) and why a few global layers fix it.
3. **Attention sinks** (StreamingLLM): why models dump attention on the first tokens, why
   evicting them breaks sliding-window inference, the fix (keep sink tokens), and learned sink
   logits (gpt-oss); link to 11 for the stability view.
4. **Sparse attention**: fixed patterns (Sparse Transformer, BigBird), and learned or dynamic
   selection: Native Sparse Attention (DeepSeek, 2025: compressed + selected + sliding
   branches, hardware-aligned blocks), MoBA (Kimi), and **DeepSeek Sparse Attention** in V3.2
   (lightning indexer with ReLU-scored FP8 heads; top-2048 key selection per query; built on
   MLA's MQA mode; dense warm-up training of the indexer with a KL loss, then sparse training;
   O(L²) indexer but O(Lk) core attention). Distinguish what each saves: FLOPs, bandwidth,
   or memory.
5. **Linear attention**: replace \(\exp(q \cdot k)\) with a feature map \(\phi(q)\cdot\phi(k)\);
   derive the recurrent form \(S_t = S_{t-1} + \phi(k_t)^\top v_t\), \(o_t = \phi(q_t) S_t\)
   (with normalizer); constant state; why quality lags (fixed-size state, no sharp retrieval).
   Decay/gating: RetNet, GLA.
6. **State-space models**: S4 in one paragraph; **Mamba** (selective SSM: input-dependent
   Δ, B, C; hardware-aware scan) and **Mamba-2** (SSD: the duality between a structured SSM
   and masked linear attention, enabling matmul-based chunked training).
7. **Delta-rule models**: DeltaNet (the state is updated toward the new key-value association:
   \(S_t = S_{t-1} + \beta_t (v_t - k_t S_{t-1})^\top k_t\) in the appropriate convention; write
   it carefully), Gated DeltaNet (adds decay gating), and their chunkwise-parallel training.
8. **Recall and the case for hybrids**: associative recall benchmarks (Zoology/Based), the
   theoretical tension between fixed state size and exact retrieval, and the empirical finding
   that a small fraction of full-attention layers restores recall. Hybrids with verified
   ratios: Jamba (Mamba + attention + MoE), MiniMax-01 (lightning attention, 7 linear : 1
   softmax), Qwen3-Next (Gated DeltaNet + gated attention, 3:1), Nemotron-H, Kimi Linear
   (KDA). Also report any counter-trend with its stated reasons (for example if MiniMax-M2
   returned to full attention; verify from the primary source before claiming).
9. **Decision guidance**: what to choose for a new long-context model under a given budget,
   what to ablate (recall-heavy evals like RULER/NIAH variants, not just perplexity), and
   open problems. This section should be useful for the reader's own project.
