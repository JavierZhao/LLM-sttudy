# Brief 12 · Pretraining data

- File: `pages/12-pretraining-data.html` · Part III · Pretraining & systems · P0 · ~60 min · body 5,500–7,500 words
- Prereqs: 01, 02. Drill module name: `data_pipeline`

## Purpose
Data quality is the biggest lever in pretraining and a frequent interview topic for pretraining
teams ("how would you build a 15T-token dataset?", "how do you choose a data mixture?"). Cover
the full pipeline with concrete, verified numbers from open reports (FineWeb, DCLM, Llama 3,
DeepSeek, OLMo, Qwen) and the methodology for making data decisions.

## Must cover
1. **Sources and scale**: Common Crawl (WARC vs WET; why re-extracting text from HTML beats WET),
   code (GitHub/Stack), math, books, papers, multilingual; how many tokens frontier models use
   (Llama 3 ~15T, DeepSeek-V3 14.8T, Qwen3 ~36T; verify each). Chinese: 预训练数据, 数据清洗,
   去重, 数据配比.
2. **The web pipeline** step by step: URL filtering; text extraction (trafilatura, resiliparse);
   language ID (fastText); heuristic quality filters (Gopher rules, C4 rules; examples and what
   they remove); repetition filters; PII removal; toxicity/safety filtering.
3. **Deduplication**: exact (hash of lines/documents, suffix arrays for substrings) and fuzzy
   (MinHash + LSH: shingles, signatures, bands × rows, the S-curve for collision probability as
   a function of Jaccard similarity; derive it). Why dedup helps (memorization, wasted compute),
   the FineWeb finding that global dedup across dumps can hurt vs per-dump dedup (verify).
4. **Model-based quality filtering**: classifier approaches (GPT-3's reference-corpus classifier;
   FineWeb-Edu's LLM-annotated educational-value classifier; DCLM's fastText classifier trained on
   OpenHermes/ELI5-style positives); the ablation evidence (verify the headline gains); risks
   (loss of diversity, benchmark overfitting).
5. **Mixtures**: domain weights and how they are chosen: small-model ablations, DoReMi
   (group DRO proxy), RegMix/data mixing laws, Llama 3's data-mix scaling experiments (verify);
   code/math upweighting; multilingual share.
6. **Annealing / mid-training**: upweighting high-quality data (and instruction-like data) while
   the LR decays; Llama 3 and OLMo 2 evidence (verify); using annealing runs to *evaluate* small
   datasets.
7. **Repetition and data constraints**: Muennighoff et al. 2023: up to ~4 epochs is nearly as
   good as fresh data; diminishing returns after; synthetic data and rephrasing (Phi-style
   textbooks, WRAP rephrasing, Kimi K2's rephrasing pipeline) with the risks (model collapse,
   homogenization).
8. **Contamination**: n-gram overlap detection, benchmark leakage, decontamination practice and
   its limits.
9. **How labs make data decisions**: proxy models, early-signal benchmarks, compute-matched
   ablations, and why perplexity alone misleads.

## Required interview questions (12–14; include these)
- Design a pipeline to turn Common Crawl into a high-quality pretraining corpus.
- Explain MinHash LSH and how you would choose bands and rows.
- How do you decide the mixture proportions of web, code, math and books?
- What is annealing / mid-training and why does it help?
- You have 2T unique high-quality tokens but budget for 8T. What do you do?
- How do you detect and prevent benchmark contamination?

## Drill: `exercises/drills/data_pipeline.py`
- `shingles(text, n)`, `minhash_signature(shingle_set, n_perm, seed)`, `lsh_candidates(signatures, bands, rows)`, `jaccard(a, b)`.
- `gopher_quality_filter(doc) -> bool` implementing a few documented Gopher rules (word count
  range, mean word length, symbol ratio, fraction of lines ending in ellipsis; cite the rule
  values in the docstring).
- Tests: near-duplicates become candidates, unrelated texts do not (fixed seeds); the filter
  accepts normal prose and rejects boilerplate examples.
