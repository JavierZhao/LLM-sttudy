# Brief 13 · Scaling laws

- File: `pages/13-scaling-laws.html` · Part III · Pretraining & systems · P0 · ~60 min · body 5,500–7,500 words
- Prereqs: 01, 04. Drill module name: `scaling`

## Purpose
Scaling laws are how labs plan compute and de-risk big runs. Interviews ask for compute-optimal
calculations on the spot and probe the Kaplan vs Chinchilla discrepancy. Make the reader able to
fit a law, compute an optimum, and critique the assumptions.

## Must cover
1. **Power laws in loss**: \(L(N)\), \(L(D)\), \(L(C)\); irreducible loss; log-log linearity;
   what is held fixed. Chinese: 缩放定律, 计算最优.
2. **Kaplan et al. 2020**: exponents, the "larger models are more sample-efficient" conclusion,
   the recommendation to grow N faster than D (\(N \propto C^{0.73}\); verify), and why it was
   wrong (fixed LR schedule/token count per run, embedding params excluded, small scales;
   Porian et al. 2024 / Pearce & Song 2024 reconciliations; verify).
3. **Chinchilla** (Hoffmann et al. 2022): three approaches (min over training curves; IsoFLOP
   profiles; parametric fit \(L = E + A/N^\alpha + B/D^\beta\) with fitted constants); result
   \(N_{opt} \propto C^{0.5}\), \(D_{opt} \propto C^{0.5}\), about 20 tokens per parameter;
   Chinchilla 70B vs Gopher 280B. Derive the optimum from the parametric form with \(C = 6ND\).
   Mention the Epoch AI replication (Besiroglu et al. 2024) and its corrected fit (verify).
4. **Beyond compute-optimal**: inference-aware scaling (Sardana & Frankle 2023) and why Llama 3
   8B is trained on ~15T tokens (~1,900 tokens/param); "overtraining" costs vs inference savings.
5. **Data-constrained scaling** (Muennighoff et al.): effective data with repeated epochs.
6. **Hyperparameter scaling laws**: DeepSeek LLM's power laws for optimal batch size and LR vs
   compute (verify the formulas); the idea of critical batch size; μP as the alternative (link 14).
7. **MoE scaling** (brief, link 08) and **downstream vs loss**: loss predicts benchmarks
   imperfectly; emergence and the metric-choice critique; Llama 3's two-step method for
   predicting downstream accuracy from compute (verify).
8. **How labs use scaling laws in practice**: fitting on small runs (range of FLOPs), IsoFLOP
   sweeps, extrapolation over 2–3 orders of magnitude, uncertainty, and "scaling law for your
   architecture change" as evidence that an idea is worth scaling (relevant to the reader's
   research).

## Required worked numbers

  using the fitted Chinchilla parametric law (compute both with python; show they differ).
- Llama-3-8B's and DeepSeek-V3's tokens per (activated) parameter.
- Training compute of Llama 3 405B via 6ND vs the paper's reported figure (verify).

## Required interview questions (12–14; include these)
- You have 1e24 FLOPs. What model size and token count would you choose, and why might you deviate?
- Why did Kaplan and Chinchilla reach different conclusions?
- Derive the compute-optimal allocation from \(L = E + A/N^\alpha + B/D^\beta\) with \(C = 6ND\).
- Why train an 8B model on 15T tokens if Chinchilla says ~160B?
- How would you use scaling laws to decide whether a new architecture is better?
- Does lower loss guarantee better downstream performance?
