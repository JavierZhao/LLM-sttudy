# Brief 14 · Optimizers, schedules & hyperparameters

- File: `pages/14-optimization.html` · Part III · Pretraining & systems · P0 · ~65 min · body 6,000–8,000 words
- Prereqs: 04, 11, 13. Drill module name: `optim`

## Purpose
The optimizer and schedule choices of real LLM runs, with their reasons: AdamW details that
matter at scale, warmup, cosine vs WSD, batch size, clipping, μP and hyperparameter transfer,
and Muon (now used at trillion-parameter scale by Kimi K2). Interviewers probe both the math
and practical debugging.

## Must cover
1. **AdamW** in full (moments, bias correction, decoupled weight decay vs L2 in Adam); typical
   LLM settings (β1 = 0.9, β2 = 0.95, ε ≈ 1e-8 or smaller, weight decay 0.1; verify examples
   from Llama 2/3 and DeepSeek); why β2 = 0.95 rather than 0.999 (faster adaptation, spike
   resistance); memory cost (2 states per param, fp32). Adafactor briefly. Chinese: 优化器,
   学习率调度, 权重衰减, 梯度裁剪.
2. **Learning-rate schedules**: warmup (why: Adam's early second-moment estimates, pre-norm
   gradients); cosine decay to ~10% of peak; **WSD** (warmup-stable-decay; MiniCPM) and why it
   is popular (continue training from the stable phase, cheap scaling-law sweeps, decay =
   annealing, link 12); DeepSeek's step decay (verified in DeepSeek-V2 §3.1.2: ×0.316 at ~60%
   and ~90% of tokens) and V3's schedule (verify).
3. **Batch size**: tokens per batch in practice (millions), batch-size warmup (DeepSeek-V2/V3
   ramps; verify numbers), the critical batch size / gradient noise scale (McCandlish et al.
   2018) and how it grows during training.
4. **Gradient clipping** (global norm 1.0), what it hides, and monitoring grad norms.
5. **μP / hyperparameter transfer** (Tensor Programs V): parametrize init and per-layer LRs so
   the optimal LR is width-independent; what changes vs standard parametrization; empirical
   use (Cerebras-GPT, MiniCPM; verify); why many labs instead fit HP scaling laws (DeepSeek LLM,
   link 13).
6. **Muon**: orthogonalized momentum updates via Newton–Schulz iteration (write the quintic
   iteration and coefficients from Keller Jordan's post / Moonlight paper, verify), applied to
   2-D hidden matrices while embeddings/heads use AdamW; why it can be more token-efficient;
   Moonlight's scaling fixes (weight decay, per-shape update RMS matching); Kimi K2's MuonClip /
   QK-clip for logit explosion (link 11). State the evidence and its limits honestly.
7. **Loss-spike playbook** (link 11): skip batches, lower LR, restart from checkpoint, check data,
   increase ε or β2 issues, monitor per-layer update/param ratios.
8. **Other optimizers in one table** (Lion, Sophia, Shampoo/SOAP, Adam-mini; verified claims
   only) and the "most don't beat well-tuned AdamW at scale" caution (cite a benchmark study if
   one exists, verify).

- Why do LLMs use β2 = 0.95?
