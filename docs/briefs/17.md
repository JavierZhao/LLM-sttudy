# Brief 17 · Inference optimization & serving

- File: `pages/17-inference-serving.html` · Part III · Pretraining & systems · P1 · ~60 min · body 5,500–7,500 words
- Prereqs: 06, 07, 08, 16. Drill module name: `speculative`

## Purpose
Architecture choices at frontier labs are increasingly driven by inference cost. Cover the
serving stack a model builder must understand: batching and memory management, quantization,
speculative decoding, prefill/decode disaggregation, and MoE serving.

## Must cover
1. **Serving metrics and the cost model** (recap 06): TTFT, TPOT, throughput, goodput under
   SLOs; cost per million tokens as a function of batch and context. Chinese: 推理优化, 量化,
   投机解码, 连续批处理.
2. **Batching**: static vs **continuous (in-flight) batching** (Orca); **PagedAttention** (vLLM:
   block tables, near-zero fragmentation, copy-on-write for beam/parallel sampling; verify the
   reported waste numbers); prefix caching / RadixAttention (SGLang); chunked prefill.
3. **Prefill/decode disaggregation** (DistServe, Splitwise; DeepSeek-V3's separate prefill and
   decode deployments with verified sizes from V3 §3.4).
4. **Quantization**: weight-only (GPTQ, AWQ; INT4/INT8), weight+activation (LLM.int8 outliers,
   SmoothQuant), FP8 inference, KV-cache quantization (KIVI); why activation outliers make W8A8
   hard; accuracy/throughput trade-offs; FP4 formats on new hardware (verify).
5. **Speculative decoding**: draft-then-verify with the rejection-sampling acceptance rule that
   preserves the target distribution (derive it); expected tokens per step as a function of
   acceptance rate α and draft length γ (Leviathan et al. formula); draft sources: small models,
   Medusa heads, EAGLE, and DeepSeek-V3's MTP module (verify the acceptance rate V3 reports).
6. **MoE serving**: expert parallelism at inference, per-expert batch sizes at decode, redundant
   experts and load-balancing (V3 §3.4), memory footprint of total params.
7. **Long-context serving**: KV memory (link 07), offloading, sparse attention (link 09).

## Required worked numbers
- Speculative decoding speedup for α = 0.6/0.8/0.9 and γ = 1..5 (expected accepted tokens;
  compute with python).
- Memory and bandwidth savings of INT4 weights for a 70B model at batch 1.
- PagedAttention: wasted KV memory with contiguous preallocation vs 16-token blocks for a given
  length distribution (illustrative, show assumptions).

## Required figures (2)
- Continuous batching timeline vs static batching.
- Speculative decoding: draft tokens, parallel verification, accept/reject and bonus token.

## Required interview questions (12–14; include these)
- Explain continuous batching and PagedAttention. What problems do they solve?
- Derive the speculative-decoding acceptance rule. Why is the output distribution unchanged?
- When does speculative decoding stop helping?
- Why is W8A8 quantization harder than W4A16? What does SmoothQuant do?
- Why separate prefill and decode onto different GPUs?
- How do you serve a 671B MoE model economically?

## Drill: `exercises/drills/speculative.py`
- `speculative_accept(p_target, q_draft, draft_tokens, generator)` implementing the
  accept/resample rule for one draft sequence, returning accepted tokens plus the correction
  or bonus token.
- `expected_tokens_per_step(alpha, gamma)`.
- Test: Monte-Carlo check that the marginal distribution of the first emitted token equals the
  target distribution (fixed seed, tolerance), and closed-form expectations.
