# Brief 18 · Evaluation

- File: `pages/18-evaluation.html` · Part III · Pretraining & systems · P1 · ~45 min · body 4,500–6,500 words
- Prereqs: 01, 06. Drill module name: `evals`

## Purpose
How LLMs are evaluated during pretraining, after post-training, and in papers, and how
evaluations go wrong. Interviewers test judgment: "is this 1-point gain real?", "why did MMLU go
up but the model feel worse?".

## Must cover
1. **Why evaluation is hard**: open-ended outputs, prompt sensitivity, contamination, saturation,
   noise. Chinese: 评测, 基准, 数据污染.
2. **Benchmark taxonomy** (with what each measures and its known issues; verify facts):
   knowledge (MMLU, MMLU-Pro), reasoning/science (GPQA Diamond, BBH), math (GSM8K, MATH/
   MATH-500, AIME), code (HumanEval, MBPP, LiveCodeBench, SWE-bench Verified), long context
   (link 10), instruction following (IFEval), chat preference (Chatbot Arena / LMArena,
   AlpacaEval LC, Arena-Hard), agentic (τ-bench, Terminal-Bench), frontier difficulty
   (Humanity's Last Exam, FrontierMath, ARC-AGI). Keep one line each.
3. **Scoring methods**: log-likelihood multiple choice (cloze vs MC formulation, length
   normalization), generative with answer extraction, pass@k with the **unbiased estimator**
   (Chen et al. 2021; derive), maj@k / self-consistency, LLM-as-judge (position, verbosity and
   self-preference biases; length-controlled win rates).
4. **Evaluation during pretraining**: early-signal benchmarks (which ones are informative at
   small scale), smoothness vs noise, using loss on held-out domains; OLMo/DataDecide-style
   methodology if verifiable.
5. **Statistics**: standard errors for accuracy on N questions (binomial), paired comparisons,
   seed variance, and why many reported gains are within noise (cite Anthropic's "Adding error
   bars to evals" or similar; verify).
6. **Contamination and leakage**: detection (n-gram overlap, perplexity on canaries, rewritten
   benchmark variants), dynamic benchmarks (LiveCodeBench), decontamination limits.
7. **Evaluating reasoning models**: sampling temperature, token budgets, pass@1 averaged over
   samples, reporting conventions.

## Required worked numbers
- Standard error of a 70% accuracy on 500 and on 14,000 questions; the minimum difference
  detectable at 95% confidence (unpaired and paired intuition).
- pass@k from n = 20 samples with c = 3 correct for k = 1, 5, 10 (compute).

## Required figures (1–2)
- Benchmark map by capability and difficulty/saturation (a simple grid SVG).

## Required interview questions (12–14; include these)
- Derive the unbiased pass@k estimator. Why not just use 1 − (1 − p̂)^k?
- A new method improves MMLU by 0.8 points. Is it real? What would you check?
- Log-likelihood vs generative evaluation: when do they disagree and why?
- What are the biases of LLM-as-judge and how do you mitigate them?
- How do you detect benchmark contamination?
- Which evals would you track during pretraining of a 7B model, and why?

## Drill: `exercises/drills/evals.py`
- `pass_at_k(n, c, k)` (numerically stable), `accuracy_ci(correct, n, z)`,
  `paired_bootstrap(a_correct, b_correct, n_boot, seed)`, and `mc_loglik_choice(logprobs_per_choice,
  lengths, normalize)` for cloze-style scoring.
- Tests: pass@k matches brute-force combinatorics; CI coverage on simulated data; bootstrap
  returns sensible intervals.
