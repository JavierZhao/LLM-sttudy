# Brief 19 · SFT, instruction tuning & distillation

- File: `pages/19-sft-distillation.html` · Part IV · Post-training · P0 · ~55 min · body 5,500–7,500 words
- Prereqs: 01, 02, 06. Drill module name: `sft`

## Purpose
How a base model becomes an assistant through supervised finetuning, how SFT data is built, and
how distillation (off-policy and on-policy) transfers capability from a stronger model, which is
how most small open models (and DeepSeek-R1's distilled models) are made.

## Must cover
1. **What SFT changes**: format and behavior more than knowledge (LIMA's superficial alignment
   hypothesis, with its limits); the objective (next-token CE on responses only). Chinese:
   监督微调, 指令微调, 知识蒸馏, 聊天模板.
2. **Chat templates and special tokens**: roles, turn delimiters, system prompts, generation
   prompt; examples (ChatML-style, Llama 3 header tokens; verify exact strings from official
   sources); the train/inference template mismatch bug; tool-call formatting.
3. **Loss masking**: only assistant tokens contribute; multi-turn masking; EOS handling.
4. **Packing**: concatenating examples to fill sequences; cross-document attention
   contamination and the fix (document-aware masks / varlen attention, position-id resets);
   loss normalization per token vs per sequence under packing (and why it biases toward long
   examples).
5. **Data**: FLAN-style task mixtures; Self-Instruct / Alpaca synthetic data; LIMA (1,000
   curated examples); Tulu 3's SFT mixture design and decontamination; quality > quantity,
   diversity, difficulty; synthetic data from stronger models; long-CoT SFT data for reasoning.
6. **Hyperparameters and pitfalls**: small LR (verify typical values, e.g. Tulu 3), few epochs,
   catastrophic forgetting and mixing in pretraining data, overfitting to style, sycophancy.
7. **Distillation** (知识蒸馏): logit KD (Hinton; forward KL on soft targets with temperature),
   sequence-level KD (train on teacher samples), on-policy distillation (GKD; student samples,
   teacher scores per token, reverse KL; MiniLLM), strong-to-weak distillation in Qwen3 and
   DeepSeek-R1's distilled Qwen/Llama models (verify sample counts: ~800K), why distillation
   beats RL for small models (R1 report; verify the claim and numbers).
8. **Rejection-sampling finetuning** (RFT / best-of-n SFT, STaR): sample, filter by a verifier
   or RM, finetune; its relation to RL (link 22–24).

## Required interview questions (12–14; include these)
- What does SFT teach the model that pretraining did not?
- Why mask the prompt tokens in the loss? What about multi-turn conversations?
- What goes wrong with naive sequence packing, and how do you fix it?
- Forward vs reverse KL for distillation: which is mode-seeking and why does it matter?
- How would you build an SFT dataset for a coding assistant from scratch?
- Why does distilling a large reasoning model beat running RL on a small model?

## Drill: `exercises/drills/sft.py`
- `build_example(messages, tokenize, template)` → (input_ids, loss_mask) given a toy tokenizer
  and a template spec (assistant turns and EOS are trained; everything else masked).
- `pack(examples, max_len)` → packed ids, position ids (reset per document), document ids,
  and `document_causal_mask(doc_ids)`.
- `kd_loss(student_logits, teacher_logits, T, kind="forward"|"reverse", mask)`.
- Tests: masks on a known conversation; packed attention mask is block-diagonal causal; KD
  matches a manual KL computation.
