# Brief 21 · Reward models & preference optimization (DPO family)

- File: `pages/21-preference-optimization.html` · Part IV · Post-training · P0 · ~65 min · body 6,000–8,000 words
- Prereqs: 19; 22 is helpful but not required (keep the RL needed here self-contained). Drill module name: `dpo`

## Purpose
The DPO derivation is one of the most frequently asked derivations in post-training interviews.
The reader must be able to derive it from the KL-regularized objective without notes, explain
its gradient, and discuss its failure modes and the zoo of variants, as well as reward modeling.

## Must cover
1. **Preference data**: pairwise comparisons (chosen/rejected), how they are collected (human,
   AI feedback), noise and annotator disagreement. Chinese: 偏好优化, 奖励模型, 直接偏好优化.
2. **Bradley-Terry reward models**: \(P(y_w \succ y_l) = \sigma(r(x,y_w) - r(x,y_l))\);
   pairwise logistic loss; architecture (LM backbone + scalar head on the final token);
   reward-margin and ensembling tricks; evaluation (accuracy on held-out pairs, RewardBench).
3. **Reward over-optimization** (Gao et al. 2022): proxy reward rises while gold reward peaks
   and falls as KL from the initial policy grows; functional forms they fit; Goodhart.
4. **The KL-regularized objective** \(\max_\pi \E[r] - \beta\,\KL(\pi \| \pi_{ref})\); derive the
   closed-form optimum \(\pi^*(y|x) = \pi_{ref}(y|x) e^{r(x,y)/\beta} / Z(x)\) (Gibbs / variational
   argument, every step).
5. **DPO derivation**: invert to \(r = \beta \log \frac{\pi^*}{\pi_{ref}} + \beta \log Z\);
   substitute into Bradley-Terry, where \(Z\) cancels; the DPO loss
   \(-\log\sigma\big(\beta \log\frac{\pi_\theta(y_w)}{\pi_{ref}(y_w)} - \beta\log\frac{\pi_\theta(y_l)}{\pi_{ref}(y_l)}\big)\);
   the gradient and its interpretation (weights examples where the implicit reward ranks the
   pair wrongly); sequence log-probs as sums of token log-probs (masking the prompt).
6. **What DPO assumes and where it breaks**: off-policy data (distribution shift from the
   policy), the reference model's role, β sensitivity, length exploitation, likelihood
   displacement (chosen log-prob can *decrease*; cite a paper analyzing it, verify), overfitting
   to deterministic preferences (IPO's argument).
7. **Variants** with one-line objectives each (verify): IPO (squared loss to a target margin),
   KTO (unpaired, prospect-theory utility), ORPO (odds-ratio term added to SFT, no reference),
   SimPO (length-normalized log-prob as reward, target margin, no reference), cDPO/robust DPO
   (label noise), online/iterative DPO (sample from the current policy, label with an RM).
8. **DPO vs PPO**: the evidence (Xu et al. 2024 "Is DPO superior to PPO?"; Tulu 3's use of
   length-normalized DPO then RLVR; Llama 3's DPO rounds); when online RL wins.

## Required interview questions (13–15; include these)
- Derive DPO from the KL-regularized RL objective.
- What does the DPO gradient do, per example?
- Why doesn't the partition function Z appear in the DPO loss?
- Why can the chosen response's likelihood decrease during DPO training?
- DPO vs PPO: when would you prefer each?
- IPO, KTO, SimPO, ORPO: what problem does each address?
- How do you train and evaluate a reward model? What is reward over-optimization?
