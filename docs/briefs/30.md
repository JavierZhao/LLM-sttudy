# Brief 30 · Qwen 1 to 3 and Qwen3-Next

## Purpose
Qwen is the most widely used open model family for research finetuning and RL (most RLVR papers
use Qwen2.5/Qwen3 bases), and Qwen3-Next is a leading hybrid-attention design. Cover the family's
evolution and the specific techniques Qwen introduced. Search for Qwen releases after your
training data (as of September 2026) and include only verified ones.

## Must cover (verify every number)
1. **Qwen 1 → 1.5 → 2** (2023–2024): vocabulary (~151K), QKV bias (and its later removal),
   GQA, long-context via YaRN + Dual Chunk Attention, Qwen2-57B-A14B MoE.
2. **Qwen2.5**: 18T tokens, sizes, Qwen2.5-Math/Coder, Qwen2.5-1M (progressive long-context
   training, inference with sparse/DCA tricks), and why Qwen2.5 bases became the RL community's
   default (and the caveat that some RLVR findings may be Qwen-specific; link 24's spurious
   rewards discussion).
3. **Qwen3** (May 2025): dense 0.6B–32B and MoE 30B-A3B / 235B-A22B (128 experts, 8 active, no
   shared expert, global-batch load balancing), QK-norm, ~36T tokens in 119 languages, three
   pretraining stages; post-training: long-CoT cold start → reasoning RL → thinking-mode fusion
   → general RL; unified thinking/non-thinking with a thinking budget; strong-to-weak
   distillation for small models. Later split into separate Instruct/Thinking releases (2507;
   verify).
4. **GSPO** (Qwen's RL algorithm; link 24) and why MoE made token-level ratios unstable.
5. **Gated attention** (Qwen team's paper: sigmoid gate on attention output; effects on
   attention sinks, massive activations and stability; verify ID and claims).
6. **Qwen3-Next** (Sep 2025): 80B total / ~3B active, hybrid layout (Gated DeltaNet : gated full
   attention = 3 : 1), ultra-sparse MoE (512 experts, 10 routed + 1 shared; verify), MTP,
   zero-centered and weight-decayed layer norm, training cost and long-context throughput claims.
7. **Later Qwen releases** up to September 2026 (search; verify), e.g. any Qwen3.5/Qwen4 architecture changes.
8. **Qwen vs DeepSeek design choices**: shared experts, attention type, balancing, RL algorithm;
   a comparison table.
