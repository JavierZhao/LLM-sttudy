Positional encoding & RoPE

P0 ~55 min Prereqs: 03 Attention

Self-attention has no notion of order, so every language model must inject it. This page derives the scheme almost every open LLM now uses, rotary position embedding (RoPE), compares it with the alternatives (sinusoidal, learned, relative bias, ALiBi, no encoding at all), and explains why the RoPE base and position rescaling are the levers of long-context extension.

What interviewers probe
  • The derivation: the RoPE score depends only on \(t-j\), in complex and matrix form; why \(q\) and \(k\) but not \(v\).
  • Back-of-envelope: the slowest wavelength for head dimension 128 at base 10,000 versus 500,000, against 8K and 128K.
  • Implementation: both pair layouts (and why mixing them silently breaks a checkpoint), the cos/sin cache, position ids under a KV cache.
  • Alternatives: ALiBi, T5 bias, NoPE, and the extrapolation evidence.
  • Long context: why models fail past their training length, extrapolation versus interpolation, why Llama 3 raised the base.

After this page you can

  • Prove that attention without positional information is permutation-equivariant, and explain how a causal decoder can still infer position.
  • Write the sinusoidal encoding and ALiBi's bias (with the slopes for \(n\) heads) and state each one's failure mode.
  • Derive RoPE's relative-position property in complex and real block-rotation form, and say why it acts after the projections and only on \(q\) and \(k\).
  • Compute RoPE wavelengths from \((d_h, b)\) and compare them with a context length.
  • Implement RoPE in both layouts and convert between them; explain partial, decoupled and hybrid RoPE/NoPE schemes and why RoPE fails beyond its training length.

Why position information is needed

Attention is permutation-equivariant

Stack the token vectors as the rows of \(X \in \R^{T \times d}\) (row-vector convention, as on page 03) and let \(P\) be any permutation matrix. With no mask, one head computes \(\operatorname{Attn}(X) = \softmax\!\big(X W^Q W^{K\top} X^\top / \sqrt{d_h}\big) X W^V\). Feed \(PX\) instead: the score matrix becomes \(P S P^\top\), softmax acts along rows so \(\softmax(P S P^\top) = P\,\softmax(S)\,P^\top\), and because \(P^\top P = I\):

\[ \operatorname{Attn}(PX) = P\,\operatorname{Attn}(X) \]
Permutation equivariance 置换等变性: shuffle the input tokens and the outputs are the same vectors, shuffled

Norms, FFNs and residual adds act on each token independently, so a whole stack with no positional encoding 位置编码 is equivariant. "Dog bites man" and "man bites dog" contain the same tokens, so the model cannot tell them apart.

From your jet tagger

Your tagger relied on exactly this property: particles in a jet have no canonical order, so you wanted equivariant attention and a permutation-invariant readout (pooling). A language model needs the opposite. Order carries meaning, so we must break the symmetry deliberately, in a way that generalizes across positions.

The causal mask leaks position

A decoder-only model adds a causal mask 因果掩码, so row \(t\) of the softmax runs over keys \(1,\dots,t\) only. The output is blind to the order of the predecessors but not to their number, which is enough to recover absolute position. Kazemnejad et al. construct it in the first layer: set \(W^K\) so every key is the same constant (all visible scores tie, so every weight is \(1/t\)), and let \(W^V\) copy a flag that is 1 only on the <bos> token. The head outputs \(1/t\), strictly monotone in \(t\), and the FFN can decode \(t\) from it. A second construction lets later layers turn this into a relative signal, a score of the form \(f_{\text{cnt}}(q,k) + f_{\text{rel}}(t-j)\) [Kazemnejad et al. 2023, Thm. 1-2, App. C]. So a causal transformer can learn positions with none supplied, which is why the NoPE results below are not absurd. A bidirectional encoder has no such leak: without positional encoding it is a bag of words [§2], your tagger's regime by design.

What a positional scheme should do

Ideally it is relative (language statistics are shift-invariant: what matters is the offset \(t-j\)), length-general (below), and cheap: no \(T\times T\) tensor, compatible with fused kernels and the KV cache of page 06. Schemes differ in where position enters: the input embeddings (sinusoidal, learned), the attention logits (Shaw, T5, ALiBi), queries and keys (RoPE), or nowhere (NoPE).

Absolute encodings: sinusoidal and learned

Sinusoidal

An absolute encoding 绝对位置编码 gives each index its own vector, added to the token embedding. The original Transformer uses a fixed one [Vaswani et al. 2017, §3.5]:

\[ PE_{t,2i} = \sin\!\big(t / 10000^{2i/d}\big), \qquad PE_{t,2i+1} = \cos\!\big(t / 10000^{2i/d}\big), \qquad i = 0, \dots, d/2 - 1 . \]

Write \(\theta_i = 10000^{-2i/d}\). Each pair \((PE_{t,2i}, PE_{t,2i+1})\) moves around the unit circle at \(\theta_i\) radians per position, a sinusoid of wavelength \(2\pi/\theta_i = 2\pi \cdot 10000^{2i/d}\). The wavelengths form a geometric progression from \(2\pi \approx 6.3\) tokens to about \(10000 \cdot 2\pi \approx 62{,}800\) tokens. Fast pairs resolve fine position but alias; slow pairs are coarse but unambiguous over long ranges, like the digits of a number in mixed scales.

The linear-offset property. For a fixed offset \(k\), \(\big(\sin\theta_i(t{+}k), \cos\theta_i(t{+}k)\big) = \big(\sin\theta_i t\cos\theta_i k + \cos\theta_i t\sin\theta_i k,\ \cos\theta_i t\cos\theta_i k - \sin\theta_i t\sin\theta_i k\big)\): the pair at \(t\) rotated by \(\theta_i k\), the same rotation for every \(t\). So \(PE_{t+k} = PE_t M_k\) for a block-diagonal rotation \(M_k\), which is why Vaswani et al. expected the model to "easily learn to attend by relative positions". Also \(PE_t \cdot PE_{t+k} = \sum_i \cos(k\theta_i)\) depends only on \(k\).

Why that does not make attention relative. With \(x_t = e_t + p_t\) (token embedding plus encoding) and \(W = W^Q W^{K\top}\), the logit expands into four terms:

\[ (e_t + p_t)\, W\, (e_j + p_j)^\top = e_t W e_j^\top + e_t W p_j^\top + p_t W e_j^\top + p_t W p_j^\top . \]

Only the last is purely positional, and \(p_t W p_j^\top\) depends on \(t-j\) alone only if the learned \(W\) commutes with the rotations \(M_k\) (then \(M_t W M_j^\top = W M_{t-j}\)). Generally it does not, and the other three terms mix content with absolute position [Su et al. 2021, Eq. 6]. The model must learn to be relative. RoPE hard-wires the missing step by putting the rotation between \(q\) and \(k\).

Learned absolute embeddings and the length limit

Replace the formula with a trainable table \(P \in \R^{L_{\max} \times d}\). BERT has 512 rows and GPT-2 has 1,024 (BERT config, GPT-2 config). Beyond \(L_{\max}\) there is no row: context length is a hard limit, and late rows are trained on fewer examples. Vaswani et al. found the two variants nearly identical: 4.92 dev perplexity and 25.7 BLEU for learned against 4.92 and 25.8 for sinusoids [Table 3, row (E)]. They kept sinusoids because they "may allow the model to extrapolate". In practice they do not: trained at \(L = 512\), perplexity is 20.05 at 512 tokens, 24.86 at 712 and 132.41 at 2,512, so a sinusoidal model cannot extrapolate "to more than a few dozen tokens beyond \(L\)" [Press et al. 2021, §2.2, App. Table 2].

Relative encodings: Shaw, T5 bias, ALiBi

Shaw et al.: learned relative embeddings

A relative encoding 相对位置编码 depends on the offset, not the index. Shaw et al. give the logit and the value an extra learned vector indexed by the clipped offset between query \(i\) and key \(j\) [Shaw et al. 2018, §3.1-3.2]:

\[ e_{ij} = \frac{x_i W^Q \big(x_j W^K + a^K_{ij}\big)^\top}{\sqrt{d_z}}, \qquad z_i = \sum_j \alpha_{ij}\big(x_j W^V + a^V_{ij}\big), \qquad a^K_{ij} = w^K_{\operatorname{clip}(j-i,\,k)} , \]

with \(\operatorname{clip}(x,k) = \max(-k, \min(k, x))\), so only \(2k+1\) vectors are learned, on the reasoning that "precise relative position information is not useful beyond a certain distance". On WMT14 En-De they gain 0.3 BLEU (base, 26.5 to 26.8) and 1.3 (big, 27.9 to 29.2) over absolute encodings, at about 7% fewer training steps per second [Table 1, §3.3]. The ablations say more: clipping distance \(k = 0\) (no position information) gives 12.5 BLEU, \(k = 1\) gives 25.5, \(k \ge 2\) about 25.8 [Table 2]; dropping \(a^V\) costs nothing, dropping \(a^K\) costs 0.5 [Table 3]. Local order matters most and the logit is where it belongs; later schemes keep only that term.

T5: a scalar bias per bucket

T5 reduces this to one learned scalar per head, added to the logit and chosen by the offset [Raffel et al. 2020, §2.1]: 32 buckets whose widths grow logarithmically up to offset 128, with all larger offsets sharing one bucket; shared across layers, separate per head. One layer is thus insensitive beyond 128 tokens, though later layers can build sensitivity by combining local information. In T5-3B some heads mask distant tokens while others attend to them [Kazemnejad et al., Fig. F.3]. The price is an \((n_h \times T \times T)\) bias added before the softmax, measured by Press et al. as considerably slower and more memory-hungry than sinusoidal encodings [§1, Fig. 2].

ALiBi: a fixed linear penalty

ALiBi (Attention with Linear Biases) replaces the table with a fixed linear function of distance [Press et al. 2021, §3]. For query \(i\) (1-indexed) over keys \(1,\dots,i\) in one head:

\[ \softmax\!\big( q_i K^\top + m \cdot [-(i-1), \dots, -2, -1, 0] \big) , \]

a logit bias of \(-m\,(i-j)\) on key \(j\). The slope \(m\) is head-specific and fixed, not learned; no positional embedding is added at the input; and the bias is not multiplied by \(1/\sqrt{d_k}\) [footnote 10]. For \(n\) heads (a power of two) the slopes are the geometric sequence starting at \(2^{-8/n}\) with the same ratio: for 8 heads \(2^{-1}, 2^{-2}, \dots, 2^{-8}\); for 16 heads it starts at \(2^{-0.5}\). Like T5 bias and RoPE, it acts on queries and keys in every layer and never on values.

Rotary position embedding (RoPE)

RoPE 旋转位置编码 [Su et al. 2021] is the position scheme of the Llama, Qwen, Mistral, DeepSeek and Gemma families and most other open models (configs in the table below). It keeps the sinusoidal idea, a rotation that depends on position, and moves it to the one place where it yields a relative score: between query and key.

The requirement and the 2-D solution

We want \(f_q, f_k\) such that the inner product depends on the two positions only through the offset [Su et al., Eq. 11]: \(\langle f_q(x_m, m), f_k(x_n, n) \rangle = g(x_m, x_n, m - n)\). In the plane, under the paper's assumptions, two facts fall out [§3.4.1, Eq. 26-30]. Setting \(m = n\) shows the product of the two norms is position-free (the paper takes each norm to be). Setting \(n = m+1\) shows the angle added at \(m+1\) minus the angle added at \(m\) is constant, so the angle is affine in position: \(f(x, m) = (Wx)\,e^{\mathrm{i} m\theta}\). In \(d_h\) dimensions, use \(d_h/2\) planes with a frequency \(\theta_i\) each; the inner product is a sum over planes, so the property survives.

Complex form and proof

Let \(q_t, k_j \in \R^{d_h}\) be a head's projected query and key (row vectors). Pair up entries: \(z^q_i = q_{2i} + \mathrm{i}\,q_{2i+1}\), \(z^k_i = k_{2i} + \mathrm{i}\,k_{2i+1}\), \(i = 0, \dots, d_h/2 - 1\). RoPE rotates pair \(i\) by an angle proportional to position:

\[ \tilde z^q_i = z^q_i\, e^{\mathrm{i}\, t \theta_i}, \qquad \tilde z^k_i = z^k_i\, e^{\mathrm{i}\, j \theta_i}, \qquad \theta_i = b^{-2i/d_h}. \]

The real inner product of two points in the plane is \(\operatorname{Re}(u \bar v)\). Summing over pairs,

\[ \tilde q_t \cdot \tilde k_j = \sum_{i} \operatorname{Re}\!\big( z^q_i e^{\mathrm{i} t\theta_i}\; \overline{z^k_i e^{\mathrm{i} j\theta_i}} \big) = \sum_{i} \operatorname{Re}\!\big( z^q_i\, \overline{z^k_i}\; e^{\mathrm{i}(t-j)\theta_i} \big) \]
The unit-modulus phases merge into \(e^{\mathrm{i}(t-j)\theta_i}\): the score depends on \(t\) and \(j\) only through \(t-j\)

Writing pair \(i\) of the query as \((u_1, u_2)\) and of the key as \((v_1, v_2)\), the real part is \((u_1 v_1 + u_2 v_2)\cos((t-j)\theta_i) + (u_1 v_2 - u_2 v_1)\sin((t-j)\theta_i)\), the form in [Chen et al. 2023, Eq. 2]. Micro-example: one pair with \(q = k = (1, 0)\) and \(\theta = 0.5\) scores \(\cos(0.5\,\delta)\): 1 at \(\delta = 0\), 0.540 at 2, \(-0.416\) at 4. A real head sums 64 such oscillations, weighted by content-dependent \(z^q_i \overline{z^k_i}\).

Real block-rotation form

With row vectors, a counter-clockwise rotation by \(\varphi\) acts as \((x, y)\,G(\varphi) = (x\cos\varphi - y\sin\varphi,\ x\sin\varphi + y\cos\varphi)\), the same as multiplying \(x + \mathrm{i}y\) by \(e^{\mathrm{i}\varphi}\):

\[ G(\varphi) = \begin{pmatrix} \cos\varphi & \sin\varphi \\ -\sin\varphi & \cos\varphi \end{pmatrix}, \qquad R_t = \operatorname{diag}\big(G(t\theta_0),\, \dots,\, G(t\theta_{d_h/2-1})\big). \]

RoPE is \(\tilde q_t = q_t R_t\), \(\tilde k_j = k_j R_j\). Two facts about 2-D rotations do all the work: \(G(\alpha) G(\beta) = G(\alpha + \beta)\) and \(G(\varphi)^\top = G(-\varphi)\). Hence

\[ R_t R_j^\top = R_{t-j}, \qquad \tilde q_t \tilde k_j^\top = q_t R_t R_j^\top k_j^\top = q_t\, R_{t-j}\, k_j^\top . \]
Relative-position property (row vectors, as on page 07). \(R_t\) is orthogonal, so \(\|\tilde q_t\| = \|q_t\|\)

Matching the paper. RoFormer uses column vectors and \(R^{\text{col}}(\varphi) = G(\varphi)^\top\), so its score is \((R_m q)^\top (R_n k) = q^\top R_{n-m} k\) [Eq. 16]. Same number, since \(G(t-j) = R^{\text{col}}(j-t)\). Sign slips are the classic whiteboard error, so say which convention you use.

Where it is applied, and why only \(q\) and \(k\)

RoPE acts after the \(W^Q, W^K\) projections, per head, in every layer, on queries and keys only. Nothing is added to the residual stream.

  • Why after the projection. Rotating the input first gives \(x_t R_t W^Q W^{K\top} R_j^\top x_j^\top\). The generic matrix \(W^Q W^{K\top}\) does not commute with \(R\), so \(R_t\) and \(R_j\) cannot merge into \(R_{t-j}\).
  • Why not values. The trick needs a dot product, which is bilinear: \(R_t\) and \(R_j\) meet and collapse. The output \(o_t = \sum_j a_{tj} v_j\) is a weighted sum, not a product, so rotating \(v_j\) by \(R_j\) would make the output depend on the absolute position of every attended token, with nothing to cancel it [Press et al., §3].

Frequencies and wavelengths

Pair \(i\) turns \(\theta_i = b^{-2i/d_h}\) radians per token and completes a turn every \(\lambda_i = 2\pi b^{2i/d_h}\) tokens: the sinusoidal frequencies with \(d = d_h\) [Su et al., §3.3]. Take \(d_h = 128\), as in Llama-3-8B and its larger siblings [Llama 3, Tab. 3]: 64 pairs, the fastest with \(\theta_0 = 1\) rad/token and \(\lambda_0 = 2\pi \approx 6.28\) tokens. Reading the spectrum: fast pairs resolve exact local offsets but alias (pair 0 makes offsets 0 and 6 look almost alike, since 6 rad \(\approx 2\pi - 0.28\)); slow pairs barely turn inside the training context (1.15° in 8K tokens at \(b = 5\times10^5\)), so they act as nearly position-independent, content-only channels. A pair whose wavelength exceeds the training length has never seen a full period, and that is where out-of-distribution angles first appear when running longer (below).

The "long-term decay" claim

Su et al. claim \(\theta_i = 10000^{-2i/d}\) gives a long-term decay: the inner product decays with relative distance [§3.3, §3.4.3]. The argument applies Abel summation to \(\operatorname{Re}\sum_i h_i e^{\mathrm{i}(m-n)\theta_i}\), \(h_i = z^q_i \overline{z^k_i}\), and plots that the resulting bound, proportional to \(\sum_i |S_i|\) with \(S_j = \sum_{i \lt j} e^{\mathrm{i}(m-n)\theta_i}\), shrinks with distance [Eq. 36-37, Fig. 2]. State it precisely: it is an upper bound for worst-case coefficients and says nothing about a trained model's scores. It can also be very loose: Chen et al. show a score of this form can be tiny on \([0, L]\) and huge beyond, calling the bound "vacuous" for that purpose [§2.2]. Trained models retrieve from far away, so the decay is at most a prior.

A cleaner statement is Men et al.'s. For a query \(q\), a similar key \(k^* = q + \epsilon\) and a random key \(k\), with i.i.d. components of standard deviation \(\sigma\): \(\mathbb{E}[q R_m k^*] - \mathbb{E}[q R_m k] = 2\sigma^2 \sum_i \cos(m\theta_i)\) [Men et al. 2024, Thm. 1], the advantage of a content match over noise at distance \(m\). Normalizing, \(C(m) = \tfrac{2}{d_h}\sum_i \cos(m\theta_i)\), for \(d_h = 128\):

The base \(b\)

The base sets the whole ladder \(\lambda_i = 2\pi b^{2i/d_h}\). Raising it slows every pair except the first: more pairs with wavelengths beyond the training context, a slower decay of \(C(m)\), and fewer pairs at short ranges. Base and context length are chosen together:

Llama 3 is the reference story. Meta "increase[d] the RoPE base frequency hyperparameter to 500,000", noting that Xiong et al. showed it effective up to 32,768 [Llama 3, §3.2], then extended context from 8K to 128K in six stages over about 800B tokens, checking short-context recovery and needle retrieval after each [§3.4.2]. Xiong et al.'s own experiment (RoPE ABF) continued Llama 2 7B on 32K-token sequences with the base raised from 10,000 to 500,000 and reached 32K-token Books perplexity 6.323, against 6.341 for position interpolation and 6.548 for plain RoPE [§4.1, Tab. 5]. One analysis puts the lower bound on the base near \(8.4\times10^4\) for 8K, \(6.4\times10^5\) for 32K and \(7.8\times10^6\) for 128K, though stacked layers blur the boundary [Men et al., Tab. 2].

Implementation: two pair layouts

The derivation pairs adjacent entries \((x_{2i}, x_{2i+1})\): the interleaved layout of the RoFormer paper, GPT-J (Hugging Face rotate_every_two), DeepSeek-V3's reference inference code and Llama 4's Hugging Face implementation (the last two use view_as_complex on trailing pairs). The other common choice is split halves, pairing \((x_i, x_{i+d_h/2})\) with rotate_half: GPT-NeoX and Hugging Face Llama. Neither is more correct. They are the same function class under a fixed per-head permutation of dimensions, which can be absorbed into \(W^Q\) and \(W^K\).

import torch

def rope_cos_sin(pos, d_h, base=10000.0):
    inv_freq = base ** (-torch.arange(0, d_h, 2, dtype=torch.float32) / d_h)   # (d_h/2,)  theta_i
    ang = pos.float()[:, None] * inv_freq[None, :]                              # (T, d_h/2) angles in fp32
    return ang.cos(), ang.sin()                                                 # the "cos/sin cache"

def rope_interleaved(x, cos, sin):            # x: (B, T, H, d_h); cos, sin: (T, d_h/2)
    cos, sin = cos[None, :, None, :], sin[None, :, None, :]                     # broadcast over B and H
    a, b = x[..., 0::2], x[..., 1::2]                                           # pair i = (x[2i], x[2i+1])
    return torch.stack((a * cos - b * sin, a * sin + b * cos), dim=-1).flatten(-2)

def rotate_half(x):
    h = x.shape[-1] // 2
    return torch.cat((-x[..., h:], x[..., :h]), dim=-1)                         # (-x2, x1)

def rope_half(x, cos, sin):                   # GPT-NeoX / Hugging Face Llama layout
    cos = torch.cat((cos, cos), dim=-1)[None, :, None, :]                       # frequency i sits at i and i + d_h/2
    sin = torch.cat((sin, sin), dim=-1)[None, :, None, :]
    return x * cos + rotate_half(x) * sin                                       # pair i = (x[i], x[i + d_h/2])

d_h = 8                                                                         # convert interleaved -> half:
perm = torch.cat((torch.arange(0, d_h, 2), torch.arange(1, d_h, 2)))            # [0, 2, 4, 6, 1, 3, 5, 7]
# x_half = x_interleaved[..., perm]; fold into weights per head:  W.view(d, n_h, d_h)[:, :, perm]
Pitfall: mixing layouts corrupts a converted checkpoint silently

Load weights trained in one layout into code for the other and nothing crashes and no value is NaN, but every pair is rotated by the wrong angle (entries \(2i, 2i{+}1\) are treated as \(i, i{+}d_h/2\), with wrong frequencies), so the model is just much worse. Hugging Face's Llama converter permutes the rows of \(W^Q\) and \(W^K\) per head (w.view(n_heads, dim1 // n_heads // 2, 2, dim2).transpose(1, 2).reshape(dim1, dim2), commented "permute for sliced rotary") [convert_llama_weights_to_hf.py]; its DeepSeek-V3 code permutes activations at runtime, then applies rotate_half [modeling_deepseek.py]. Defend with a logits comparison on a fixed prompt against the reference implementation.

Cost, precision and the KV cache

  • Compute is negligible. Rotating a \(d_h\)-vector costs 6 FLOPs per pair, \(3d_h = 384\) for \(d_h = 128\), against \(2 d\, d_h \approx 1.05\)M for the projection that produced it: 0.04%. It is elementwise and usually fused into the attention kernel. The cos/sin cache for 131,072 positions at \(d_h = 128\) in fp32 is \(131072 \cdot 64 \cdot 2 \cdot 4\) B = 64 MiB, shared by all layers and heads.
  • Compute angles in fp32. bfloat16 has 8 significant bits, so near 8,192 representable numbers are 64 apart and an angle of 8,192 rad is meaningless. Hugging Face forces fp32 for the angles regardless of autocast (comment: "to really force fp32") [modeling_llama.py]; cast cos and sin to the activation dtype only after the trig.
  • KV cache. The cache stores keys already rotated by their absolute position, so a decode step rotates only the new \(q\) and \(k\), at position \(=\) current length (page 06); MLA does the same with \(k^R\) (page 07). Position ids must continue from the cache length in decode and chunked prefill (restarting a chunk at 0 is a silent bug). Rotations compose, so moving a cached key by \(\delta\) positions is one more \(R_\delta\).
# decode step with a KV cache: rotate only the new token, at its absolute position
pos = torch.tensor([cache_len])                      # NOT 0: the token's index in the full sequence
cos, sin = rope_cos_sin(pos, d_h)
q = rope_half(q_new, cos, sin)                       # (B, 1, n_h, d_h)
k = rope_half(k_new, cos, sin)                       # stored already rotated
k_cache = torch.cat([k_cache, k], dim=1)             # (B, S+1, n_kv, d_h);  v_cache gets v_new unrotated

Partial RoPE and decoupled RoPE

Partial RoPE rotates only the first \(r\) of \(d_h\) dimensions and leaves the rest untouched, with frequencies \(\theta_i = b^{-2i/r}\) for the rotated slice. GPT-NeoX-20B applies rotary embeddings "only to the first 25% of embedding vector dimensions", following GPT-J, because "initial experiments indicate that this strikes the best balance of performance and computational efficiency" [Black et al. 2022, §2.1.1; rotary-pct 0.25 in App. B]: with \(6144/64 = 96\)-dim heads, 24 rotated dims (GPT-J: 64 of 256, config). GLM-4.5 rotates half of each head, Qwen3-Next a quarter and MiniMax-M2 64 of 128 (base table). A plausible benefit (our reading, not reported by GPT-NeoX) is position-free content channels.

Decoupled RoPE in MLA is partial RoPE by necessity: rotating the compressed content keys would put \(R_{t-j}\) between \(q\) and \(W^{UK}\) and break weight absorption. DeepSeek keeps the 128-dim content path unrotated and adds a 64-dim rotated path (per-head \(q^R\), one shared cached \(k^R\)): RoPE covers 64 of 192 score dimensions (page 07).

Rotary position embedding
Problem
Absolute encodings added at the input do not yield a relative score (four-term expansion above), and bias schemes need a \(T\times T\) tensor and a kernel that supports it.
Idea
Rotate each 2-D pair of \(q\) and \(k\) by an angle linear in position, with a geometric ladder \(\theta_i = b^{-2i/d_h}\).
Why it works
Rotations are orthogonal and commute, so \(R_t R_j^\top = R_{t-j}\): the score depends only on the offset, with no parameters. It acts before the attention kernel, so any fused kernel works, and cached keys are rotated once.
Evidence
Modest and mixed. RoFormer: 27.5 vs 27.3 BLEU on WMT14 En-De, faster MLM convergence than BERT [Su et al., Tab. 1, §4.2]. In Press et al.'s test rotary matches sinusoidal in-distribution (20.07 vs 20.05) and extrapolates badly (31.58 at 2,512). GPT-NeoX chose it on "positive prior experiences".
Cost
Poor extrapolation without rescaling (perplexity above \(10^3\) just past the training length for LLaMA-7B [Chen et al., Tab. 1]), a base to co-design with the context, the layout hazard, and no weight absorption in MLA.
Used by
Llama, Qwen, Mistral, DeepSeek, Gemma, GLM, OLMo (base table); partial in GPT-NeoX, GLM-4.5, Qwen3-Next.

Why did RoPE win? Our assessment: not for dramatic in-distribution gains (the evidence is thin) but because it is parameter-free, composes with the KV cache and fused kernels, and exposes knobs (base, position scaling) that extend a pretrained model's context with modest continued training (PI: about 1,000 steps [Chen et al., Abstract]). Every extension method on page 10 is built on it.

NoPE and RoPE/NoPE hybrids

No positional encoding

Kazemnejad et al. tested the causal-mask argument: decoder-only models with five schemes (sinusoidal, T5 bias, ALiBi, rotary, none), ~107M parameters, trained from scratch on ten algorithmic and reasoning tasks (copy, reverse, addition, sorting, parity, ...) at lengths up to \(L = 20\) and tested up to \(2L\). Aggregate mean reciprocal rank on length generalization: NoPE 0.69, T5 bias 0.55, ALiBi 0.50, rotary 0.33, absolute 0.22 [Fig. 1]. In-distribution all fit equally well, and NoPE's attention resembles T5's relative bias [§4-5]. In ~1B code models (1,024-token context) rotary's perplexity "explodes" past the training length while NoPE and ALiBi hold to about twice that [§7].

The caveats: small models, synthetic tasks, tiny training lengths. At 8B on standard data, Cohere's controlled comparison found pure NoPE worse: validation loss 1.58 vs 1.52 for RoPE and MMLU 47.6 vs 48.6, though its needle score at 65K was close (9.03 vs 9.82) [Yang et al. 2025, Tab. 2]. Pure NoPE is not shipped at scale; the action is in hybrids.

Interleaving RoPE and NoPE layers

Yang et al. (Cohere) saw that NoPE layers retrieve well (high attention mass on the needle) while RoPE layers have a strong recency bias. Interleaved, they self-organize into a "division of labor": RoPE layers aggregate local information, NoPE layers retrieve from far away [§2.2.3]. Their design, RNope-SWA, alternates three sliding-window 滑动窗口 RoPE layers (window 4,096) with one full-attention NoPE layer and needs no RoPE base scaling (the RoPE baseline uses 1M at 32K and 8M at 128K) [§3-4]. At 5T tokens it is on par or better on most short benchmarks (MMLU 59.5 vs 57.5; ARC-E lower, 82.5 vs 84.6) and degrades much less at length: RULER retrieval at 256K 74.8 vs 57.1 [Tab. 4, 6]. Three quarters of its layers also cache only a window (page 09). Cohere's Command A uses this recipe, 3:1 sliding-window RoPE to full-attention NoPE [Command A, §2.3].

Llama 4's iRoPE. Meta describes "interleaved attention layers without positional embeddings" plus "inference time temperature scaling of attention to enhance length generalization", and gives Scout a 10M-token context [Meta, Llama 4 announcement]. Scout's config and the Hugging Face implementation give the details:

  • 48 layers with no_rope_layers \(= [1,1,1,0]\) repeated 12 times: every fourth layer skips RoPE. RoPE base 500,000.
  • RoPE layers use chunked attention (chunk size 8,192), so they never see angles beyond that range; NoPE layers use full attention and carry the long range.
  • NoPE layers scale queries by \(1 + 0.1 \ln\!\big(1 + \lfloor (p+1)/8192 \rfloor\big)\) at position \(p\): 1 for the first 8,191 positions, about 1.48 at 1M and 1.72 at 10.5M. QK-norm applies on the RoPE layers only [modeling_llama4.py], [configuration_llama4.py].

A softmax over \(N\) keys with bounded logits spreads its mass thinly as \(N\) grows (next section), and a factor growing like \(\ln N\) counteracts that. This is our interpretation, not a rationale Meta states. See also page 29.

Command A (3:1), Llama 4 (Scout config: 1 in 4).

Length generalization: extrapolation versus interpolation

Extrapolation 长度外推 means running a model on inputs longer than any it trained on. Interpolation 位置插值 means squeezing the longer range into the range the model knows. RoPE models fail at extrapolation for two separable reasons.

Reason 1: angles the network never saw

The score's dependence on offset \(s = t - j\) is \(a(s) = \operatorname{Re}\sum_i h_i e^{\mathrm{i} s\theta_i}\), a sum of sinusoids with content-dependent coefficients, and training only evaluates it at \(s \in [0, L]\). Trigonometric functions are a universal approximator, so nothing keeps \(a(s)\) small outside that range: Chen et al. fit one on \([0, 2048]\) with \(d = 128\) and it stays within \([-1, 1]\) inside and exceeds 8,000 outside [§2.2, Fig. 2]. In practice LLaMA-7B trained at 2,048 has PG19 perplexity 7.20 at 2,048 and above \(10^3\) at 4,096 [Tab. 1]. The slow pairs (Figure 2) meet unseen phases first: at \(b = 10^4\), \(d_h = 128\), a model trained on 4,096 tokens has seen slowest-pair angles up to \(4096\,\theta_{63} = 0.47\) rad; at 16K it needs 1.9 rad.

Reason 2: attention dilution

Even with perfect positions, a softmax over more keys spreads mass more thinly. If one relevant key has logit advantage \(\Delta\) over \(N-1\) equal distractors, its weight is \(e^{\Delta}/(e^{\Delta} + N - 1)\): for \(\Delta = 8\) (\(e^8 \approx 2981\)), 0.42 at \(N = 4{,}096\) but 0.022 at \(N = 131{,}072\). A model calibrates its sharpness to the lengths it trained on, which motivates attention-temperature fixes such as YaRN's scale and Llama 4's log-scaling.

Common misconceptions

  • "RoPE adds a positional embedding to the input." It adds nothing. It rotates \(q\) and \(k\) inside every attention layer; the residual stream and \(v\) are untouched.
  • "RoPE encodes only relative position." Each vector is rotated by its absolute index; only the resulting score depends on the offset alone.
  • "Long-term decay means RoPE models cannot retrieve from far away." The decay is an upper bound for worst-case coefficients, which Chen et al. call vacuous for extrapolation. Trained models retrieve at 128K.
  • "Raising the base is a config change." It changes every pair but the first and needs continued training; a checkpoint run with the wrong base degrades silently, like the wrong layout.
  • "Interleaved and rotate_half are different position schemes." They are the same function up to a fixed per-head permutation of \(q\) and \(k\) dimensions; the bug is loading weights into the wrong one.
  • "ALiBi extrapolates, so it is best for long context." It extrapolates perplexity, largely through its recency prior; downstream it is mid-pack and its penalty is unbounded.
  • "NoPE means the model has no position information." The causal mask leaks it. Pure NoPE lags at scale, but NoPE layers inside hybrids ship in production models.

Interview questions

Warm-up Why does a transformer need positional information? Does a causal decoder strictly need it?

Self-attention is permutation-equivariant: \(\operatorname{Attn}(PX) = P\operatorname{Attn}(X)\), since the score matrix becomes \(PSP^\top\) and softmax acts row-wise. FFNs, norms and residuals are per-token, so the stack cannot tell "dog bites man" from "man bites dog" up to a shuffle. Order must be injected.

A causal decoder does not strictly need it. Row \(t\) sees exactly \(t\) keys, so a head with tied scores averages the prefix; with a value channel flagging BOS the output is \(1/t\), and the FFN can decode absolute position (Kazemnejad et al., Thm. 1). Bidirectional encoders have no such leak and become bags of words. In practice everyone supplies position explicitly, or uses NoPE only in some layers.

Warm-up Calculate: ALiBi with 8 heads. What are the slopes, and what does the steepest head add for query 5 attending to key 2?

Slopes: geometric, starting at \(2^{-8/n}\) with the same ratio, so \(1/2, 1/4, \dots, 1/256\). The bias \(-m(i-j)\) is added to the logits after \(qK^\top\), before softmax, unscaled by \(1/\sqrt d\). For \(i=5, j=2\) the distance is 3: the steepest head (\(m=1/2\)) adds \(-1.5\), the flattest (\(m=1/256\)) adds \(-0.0117\). Slopes are fixed, and nothing is added to the input embeddings. One nat of penalty sits at distance \(1/m\), so the heads have soft windows of about 2 to 256 tokens.

Warm-up Why apply RoPE to queries and keys but not to values?

The trick needs a dot product, which is bilinear: \(\tilde q_t\tilde k_j^\top = qR_tR_j^\top k^\top\), and the rotations meet and collapse into \(R_{t-j}\). The output \(o_t=\sum_ja_{tj}v_j\) is a weighted sum, not a product; rotating \(v_j\) by \(R_j\) would make the output depend on the absolute position of every attended token, with nothing to cancel it. Likewise RoPE must follow the \(W^Q, W^K\) projections, or \(W^QW^{K\top}\) would sit between the rotations.

Core Derive RoPE's relative-position property.

Complex form. Pair entries: \(z^q_i=q_{2i}+\mathrm{i}\,q_{2i+1}\), likewise \(z^k_i\); RoPE sets \(\tilde z^q_i=z^q_ie^{\mathrm{i}t\theta_i}\), \(\tilde z^k_i=z^k_ie^{\mathrm{i}j\theta_i}\). The real inner product of two points is \(\operatorname{Re}(u\bar v)\), so the score is \(\sum_i\operatorname{Re}\big(z^q_i\overline{z^k_i}e^{\mathrm{i}(t-j)\theta_i}\big)\): only \(t-j\) appears.

Matrix form (row vectors). \(G(\varphi)=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}\cos\varphi&\sin\varphi\\-\sin\varphi&\cos\varphi\end{smallmatrix}\right)\) rotates counter-clockwise; \(R_t=\operatorname{diag}(G(t\theta_i))\). Since \(G(\alpha)G(\beta)=G(\alpha+\beta)\) and \(G(\varphi)^\top=G(-\varphi)\): \(R_tR_j^\top=R_{t-j}\), so \(\tilde q_t\tilde k_j^\top=q_tR_tR_j^\top k_j^\top=q_tR_{t-j}k_j^\top\). In the paper's column convention this reads \(q^\top R_{n-m}k\).

Core What does the RoPE base control? Why did Llama 3 raise it to 500,000?

It sets the geometric ladder of frequencies \(\theta_i=b^{-2i/d_h}\), i.e. wavelengths \(2\pi b^{2i/d_h}\). Raising \(b\) slows every pair but the first, pushing wavelengths beyond the training context and slowing the decay of the content-match advantage. For \(d_h=128\) the slowest wavelength is 54K tokens at \(b=10^4\) and 2.56M at \(5\times10^5\).

Llama 3 needed 128K, so Meta raised the base to 500,000 (citing Xiong et al., who showed it works to 32K) and extended context from 8K in six stages over ~800B tokens. Costs: it changes every pair, so it needs continued training, and fewer pairs stay short-wavelength (35 of 64 below 8K instead of 50).

Core Calculate: for a 128-dimensional head, the slowest RoPE wavelength at base 10,000 and at base 500,000, and how many pairs complete a full turn within 8K and within 128K tokens.

\(\lambda_{63}=2\pi b^{126/128}\). At \(b=10^4\): \(2\pi\cdot10^{3.9375}\approx2\pi\cdot8{,}660\approx54{,}400\) tokens. At \(b=5\times10^5\): \(2\pi\cdot10^{5.6099}\approx2\pi\cdot407{,}300\approx2.56\)M.

Pair \(i\) completes a turn within \(L\) iff \(2\pi b^{2i/128} \lt L\), i.e. \(i \lt 64\log_b(L/2\pi)\). At \(b=10^4\): \(i \lt 49.8\) at 8K, so 50 of 64 pairs; at 128K the bound exceeds 63, so all 64. At \(b=5\times10^5\): \(i \lt 35.0\) at 8K (35 pairs), \(i \lt 48.5\) at 128K (49 pairs). The other 29 (8K) or 15 (128K) pairs never complete a turn: near-constant channels within the context.

Core Interleaved versus rotate-half RoPE: are they equivalent? What breaks if you mix them up?

Interleaved pairs \((x_{2i},x_{2i+1})\); rotate-half pairs \((x_i,x_{i+d_h/2})\); pair \(i\) gets \(\theta_i\) in both. With \(\text{perm}[s\cdot d_h/2+i]=2i+s\): \(\text{RoPE}_{\text{half}}(x[\text{perm}])=\text{RoPE}_{\text{int}}(x)[\text{perm}]\). Scores are identical when \(q\) and \(k\) are permuted alike, and the permutation folds into the output columns of \(W^Q\) and \(W^K\) per head (not \(W^V\)).

Running weights from one layout through the other's code rotates the wrong pairs with the wrong frequencies: no crash, no NaN, just a much worse model. That is why HF's Llama converter permutes \(W^Q,W^K\) and its DeepSeek-V3 code permutes activations at runtime. Guard with a logits comparison against the reference.

Core RoPE versus ALiBi: how do they differ, and which extrapolates better in practice?

Both are relative, act on \(q,k\) in every layer, and skip \(v\). ALiBi adds a fixed \(-m_h\cdot\text{distance}\) to the logits (a recency prior, no parameters, but the kernel must support a bias). RoPE rotates \(q\) and \(k\), so the score is an oscillating function of offset whose shape is learned through content coefficients, with no extra tensor.

ALiBi extrapolates far better as is: trained at 512, perplexity 18.41 at 2,512 versus 31.58 for rotary (Press et al.). But that is partly the recency prior (a window mask reproduces it), downstream length generalization is mid-pack (MRR 0.50 vs NoPE 0.69), and its penalty is unbounded. RoPE can be retrofitted (base increase, PI, YaRN) with modest continued training, which is why it dominates practice.

Core What are partial RoPE and decoupled RoPE, and why use them?

Partial RoPE rotates only the first \(r\) dims of each head, with \(\theta_i=b^{-2i/r}\), and leaves the rest position-free. GPT-NeoX-20B rotates 25% of each 96-dim head (24 dims), following GPT-J, reporting the best performance/compute balance in initial experiments; GLM-4.5, Qwen3-Next and MiniMax-M2 also rotate only part of each head.

Decoupled RoPE (MLA) is forced: rotating the compressed content keys would put \(R_{t-j}\) between \(q\) and \(W^{UK}\), breaking weight absorption. MLA keeps the content path unrotated and adds a 64-dim rotated path (per-head \(q^R\), one shared cached \(k^R\)). See page 07.

Core With a KV cache, do you cache rotated or unrotated keys, and what position ids do you use?

Rotated: each key is rotated once by its absolute position, so a decode step rotates only the new \(q\) and \(k\); values are never rotated. The new token's position id is the current sequence length, not 0, and a chunked prefill continues from the tokens already processed. A shared prefix must occupy identical positions, since stored keys carry their rotations. MLA does the same with \(k^R\).

Deep-dive Why is RoPE incompatible with MLA's weight absorption?

Absorption folds the key up-projection into the query: \(q\cdot(c_jW^{UK})^\top=(qW^{UK\top})\cdot c_j\), so one absorbed query is dotted with every cached latent. With RoPE on the decompressed keys the score becomes \(qR_{t-j}W^{UK\top}c_j^\top\): the position-dependent \(R_{t-j}\) sits between \(q\) and \(W^{UK}\) and changes with every cached position \(j\), so no single absorbed query exists. You would need one per \(j\), as costly as decompressing every key. The fix is decoupled RoPE: an unrotated absorbable content path plus a small rotated path with a shared cached key. See page 07.

Deep-dive Can a causal decoder learn positions with no positional encoding? Would you use NoPE?

Yes in principle: row \(t\) sees exactly \(t\) keys, so a uniform head over a BOS-flag value outputs \(1/t\), the FFN decodes absolute position, and later layers derive relative terms (Kazemnejad et al., Thm. 1-2). Empirically NoPE beat RoPE, ALiBi and T5 bias on length generalization in ~107M models on synthetic tasks (MRR 0.69 vs 0.33 for rotary).

I would not use pure NoPE for a frontier model: at 8B Cohere found validation loss 1.58 vs 1.52 and lower MMLU. I would consider the hybrid: local RoPE layers plus periodic full-attention NoPE layers (Command A 3:1, Llama 4 1 in 4), which gives long-range retrieval, no out-of-distribution angles in the RoPE layers and a smaller KV cache. The evidence is one ablation (Cohere) plus shipped models.

Deep-dive Why do RoPE models fail beyond their training length, and how do extrapolation and interpolation differ?

Two causes. (1) Unseen phases: the score's dependence on offset \(s\) is \(\sum_ih_ie^{\mathrm{i}s\theta_i}\), trained only for \(s\le L\), with nothing bounding it beyond. Chen et al. fit curves within \([-1,1]\) on \([0,2048]\) that reach over 8,000 outside; LLaMA-7B goes from perplexity 7.20 to above \(10^3\) at 4K. Slow pairs hit unseen angles first. (2) Attention dilution: with more keys and bounded logits the softmax flattens (\(\Delta=8\): weight 0.42 at 4K keys, 0.022 at 128K).

Extrapolation runs at unseen positions. Interpolation maps the longer range into the trained one: PI uses \(f(x,mL/L')\), so all angles stay in range (error bound ~600 times tighter than extrapolation's; ~1,000 fine-tuning steps), at the cost of squeezing local resolution. Raising the base (ABF) slows all pairs instead. NTK-aware and YaRN mix the two by frequency (page 10).

Deep-dive Design: your model was pretrained at 8K with RoPE base 500,000 and must now serve 128K. What do you do, and what do you check?

Recipe. Attention cost is quadratic, so train long only at the end: staged continued pretraining (Llama 3: six increments, ~800B tokens), raising the base or rescaling frequencies (PI/YaRN, page 10), a long-sequence mix plus the short mix so nothing regresses, and document masking so packed documents do not attend across boundaries (Llama 3 found it important at long lengths).

Checks. Short-context benchmarks fully recovered; needle-in-a-haystack and RULER at every length up to 128K, not perplexity alone (a small base gives low perplexity but broken retrieval). Do not trust base bounds blindly: one analysis wants ~\(7.8\times10^6\) for 128K, above 500K, so verify by retrieval. Trade-offs: a larger base helps reach but costs short-range resolution; PI needs few steps but slightly degrades the original window.

Cheat sheet

  • Attention is permutation-equivariant, \(\operatorname{Attn}(PX)=P\operatorname{Attn}(X)\). A causal mask leaks order (row \(t\) sees \(t\) keys), so NoPE can work; encoders without PE are bags of words.
  • Sinusoidal: wavelengths \(2\pi\) to \(10000\cdot2\pi\), \(PE_{t+k}=PE_tM_k\), but added at the input so the logit is not relative. Learned tables: hard \(L_{\max}\) (BERT 512, GPT-2 1,024).
  • ALiBi: bias \(-m_h(i-j)\), slopes \(2^{-8h/n}\) (8 heads: \(1/2\) to \(1/256\)), fixed, no \(1/\sqrt d\); best perplexity extrapolation, unbounded penalty. T5 bias: learned scalar per head, 32 buckets. Shaw: \(2k+1\) clipped vectors.
  • RoPE (row vectors): \(\tilde q_t=q_tR_t\), \(R_t=\operatorname{diag}(G(t\theta_i))\), \(\theta_i=b^{-2i/d_h}\), \(R_tR_j^\top=R_{t-j}\Rightarrow\tilde q_t\tilde k_j^\top=q_tR_{t-j}k_j^\top\). Complex: \(\sum_i\operatorname{Re}(z^q_i\overline{z^k_i}e^{\mathrm{i}(t-j)\theta_i})\). On \(q,k\) only, after projection, per head, every layer.
  • Wavelength \(\lambda_i=2\pi b^{2i/d_h}\). \(d_h=128\): slowest 54.4K tokens (\(b=10^4\)) vs 2.56M (\(5\times10^5\)); pairs with \(\lambda<8\)K: 50 vs 35 of 64; with \(\lambda<128\)K: 64 vs 49.
  • Bases: 10K (Llama 1-2, GPT-NeoX, DeepSeek-V3), 500K (Llama 3/4), 1M (Qwen, Mistral v0.3, GLM-4.5, Gemma 3 global), 10M (Qwen3-Next). Llama 3: 8K to 128K in six stages, ~800B tokens.
  • Layouts: interleaved \((x_{2i},x_{2i+1})\) vs half \((x_i,x_{i+d/2})\); \(\text{perm}[s\,d/2+i]=2i+s\). Mixing them silently breaks checkpoints. Angles in fp32; the cache stores rotated keys; position id = cache length.
  • Cost: 6 FLOPs per pair (0.04% of the projection); cos/sin cache for 128K, fp32: 64 MiB. Partial RoPE: GPT-NeoX 25%, GLM-4.5 50%; MLA: 64-dim rotated path.
  • Length failure: unseen phases (slow pairs) plus attention dilution (\(\Delta=8\): 0.42 at 4K keys, 0.022 at 128K). PI scales positions by \(L/L'\); ABF raises the base.
  • Hybrids: 3 local RoPE + 1 full NoPE layer (Command A); Llama 4 Scout: every 4th of 48 layers NoPE.
